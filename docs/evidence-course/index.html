Detection and PPE

1. Detection

Detection can come from a risk profile, a scan, a dog alert or an officer's suspicion. Write down what you saw and when, photograph the scene as found, and note who was present.

Do not touch. Do not open. Until it is recorded.

2. Protect yourself

Open each item to see why it matters.

Never taste or smell a substance. Some powders, such as synthetic opioids, are dangerous in tiny amounts. Know your agency's naloxone rules, work with a colleague, and keep the area ventilated.

Gathering and sampling

Put the sampling steps in the correct order. Use the arrows, then check.

    Remember: follow your laboratory's random sampling plan so the sample represents the whole load. Never reuse tools. Never mix samples from different packages unless your procedure says so.
    Field test kits are presumptive only. They tell you what to suspect, not what to prove.

    Seal and store

    Seal in tamper-evident bags, sign across the seal, write the date and time, and use a unique number. Keep a reference sample if your procedure allows it. Store evidence in a locked room with an access log; only authorised people go in.

    Drugs and money seizure

    Drugs and cash are often found together. Treat each as separate evidence, linked in one case file.

    EU reference: travellers carrying 10,000 euros or more must declare it (Regulation (EU) 2018/1672). Undeclared or suspicious cash can be detained under the law that applies in your country.
